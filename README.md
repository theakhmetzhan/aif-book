# EVIDENCE · A Casebook of Artificial Intelligence

A 3D pop-up casebook of AI fundamentals, lying open on a detective's desk at night, in front of the evidence board used to plan it. At desk level the paper exhibits tell the story; lean over the book and the pages beneath give the full explanation.

Endterm project for AI Fundamentals (AIF1303), QAIRU, Fall 2026, by Akhmetzhan Turlybek. This edition contains Cases I–III.

**Read it:** https://theakhmetzhan.github.io/aif-book/

## Controls
- **Look around:** drag (one finger on a phone).
- **Come closer:** scroll, or pinch. It zooms toward whatever is under the pointer.
- **Move sideways:** right-drag, shift-drag, or drag with two fingers.
- **Turn a page:** click or tap the page, or press ← → (Home and End jump to the covers).
- **Views:** the words at the bottom right, in two groups. For the book: Read (R) leans over the open spread; Watch (W) brings your eyes level with the pages, so only the pop-ups show; Top (T) looks straight down, so they line up flat. For the room: Desk (H); Room (O, or Escape), the whole board and desk, where it opens; Board (B), the evidence board square on.
- **Read on a phone:** it frames one page. Swipe sideways to slide to the other open page.
- **Keyboard camera:** ↑ ↓ tilt, A D orbit, + − zoom.
- **Sound:** M, or the words at the bottom left. Sound starts after your first click: light rain at the window, distant thunder after the lightning, the desk clock, and the pages.
- **Screen readers:** the text of the open spread is mirrored into the page as plain HTML and announced when the spread changes.

## How it is built
- **Case:** two cloth boards and a stiff spine strip of fixed length, joined by flexible joints. Shut, the strip stands against the block. Open, the case lies flat on the desk and stays put: the strip lies between the boards, each stack of pages sits on its board, and the crease between the top pages crosses the strip as pages turn. Both top pages roll down into the crease, and the leaves rise out of the spine in a fan, each bend nested inside the one above, so the section is paper all the way to the cloth.
- **Pages:** stiff card, always the same thickness, that turns about the back of the block. The stacks shear toward the crease, as real paper does. Every pop-up piece is a rigid card hinged to its page by a glued tab.
- **Folding:** all pieces on a page share one fold angle, set by the angle between the two pages of the spread, so they only rise as it opens. They stay inside their half of that wedge, so paper never passes through paper.
- **Collision check:** `scripts/check/collide.mjs` runs every page turn and checks every pop-up and page vertex against every page. `scripts/check/motion.mjs` drives the book with its own page spring at 60 fps and fails unless every leaf's fore-edge slides with the opening, together, with no threshold. `scripts/check/book.mjs` measures the book through every turn: the gaps the eye sees (air shut in by the book and the desk, air under the paper at either end of the book, the inside of the spine showing through the block, paper poking through the cloth, a board off the desk, a dark strip at the gutter), the strip's length, page thickness, that the spine stays one closed section, and that no sheet or pop-up jumps or reverses. `scripts/check/shots.mjs` captures every rest state and every turn in eighths, from desk level, from above and from each camera an owner complaint came from, at 1920x1080 with a 3x render of the spine and gutter: those frames are looked at, one by one.
- **Sound:** rain and thunder stay put at the window whatever the camera does. The clock and the book keep a gentle sense of place: they follow the camera slowly, within a few dB and part of the way to one side, so a fast move cannot jump. Every page and both covers make the one same sound. `scripts/check/sound.mjs` flies the camera rig through hard zooms and orbits while rendering the audio offline, and fails if the camera reaches the rain or thunder at all, or the clock or the book moves too far or too fast.
- **Views:** one camera flies between them, and a view can bring its own lens, eased like the rest, so Watch and Top are real camera moves rather than cuts. Top looks through a long lens from five metres up, which holds every pop-up within a few pixels of its foot. The first touch of a drag or scroll hands back the everyday lens at the same picture size. `scripts/check/views.mjs` runs each view and each stroke in WebKit and Chromium: no page surface in Watch, no pop-up off its foot in Top, Board square on and whole, bare labels a thumb can hit at every phone width, a swipe that slides and eases, a tap that turns, a drag that still orbits. Each rule also runs against a camera that breaks it.
- **Screen shape:** the picture takes its shape from the canvas's own box before every frame, so it cannot be shown stretched; its buffers follow once the box has held still for a few frames, so a sliding address bar does not remake them on every frame. `scripts/check/viewport.mjs` builds a phone's window, where 100vh and the visible height differ, and measures a circle in WebKit and Chromium through the address bar and rotation.
- **Speed:** one last pass adds the glow, tone-maps and lays on the film, so the picture is multisampled once. Everything that shows later goes to the GPU behind the loader, with one rehearsal of every pop-up, so no spread stops mid-turn for its first upload; the canvases are then let go. On a phone the shadow maps stay still while only the camera moves: the lamp's follows the pages, the window's follows the paper on the board. The rain slows to 15 updates a second while the view sweeps, and a phone at rest draws every other frame. `src/resolution.js` steps a phone's resolution down when a run of moving frames is slow, back up in a still moment, and leaves it alone if pixels turn out not to be the cause. `scripts/check/perf.mjs` plays one route through every view in WebKit and Chromium, at a phone's size and on a desktop, and reports frame times, stages, GPU passes, draws, triangles, memory and garbage; it fails on what does not depend on the machine. No real phone has run it: its milliseconds are this machine's.
- **Figure data:** the data in `src/data/` is generated and checked in Python, in `scripts/verify/`.

Develop: `npm i && npm run dev`. Third-party assets and sounds are credited in `public/assets/CREDITS.md` and `public/audio/CREDITS.md`. The fonts (SIL Open Font License) are served from `public/fonts/`.
